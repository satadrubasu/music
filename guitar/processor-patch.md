## Home Patch test on Blackstar V4 as mini PA speaker for Stage emulation
To make your Blackstar ID:Core V4 20 mimic a stage PA speaker as closely as possible while dialing in your patches, you must treat the Blackstar strictly as a neutral, full-range monitor. Because stage PAs are full-range systems, you must keep the Valeton GP-200's Cabinet Simulation / IRs turned ON.

1. The Correct Cable Hookup
Do not plug your Valeton into the standard front instrument input. This adds the Blackstar's internal preamp coloring to your sound.
* Run a cable from the Left/Mono Output of the Valeton GP-200.
* Connect it to the Line In / Aux In (3.5mm jack) on the top control panel of your Blackstar.
* Note: You will need a 1/4" mono jack to 3.5mm mini-jack cable or adapter for this.
This completely bypasses the Blackstar’s volume, gain, EQ, and voice knobs. The Blackstar is now acting purely as a power amp and a set of stereo full-range speakers, exactly like a mini PA system.




The reason your tones change so drastically is because you are moving between three completely different audio environments.
   A patch created on headphones will almost never sound right when plugged into a guitar amplifier or a PA system.
   Here is exactly why this happens and how to set up your Valeton GP-200 to sound great in all three situations.

## Why Your Tones Change Across Devices
* Audio-Technica Headphones: These deliver a flat, neutral, and wide stereo image directly into your ears. You hear every tiny detail, including the room mic simulations of the Valeton's Cabinet IRs.
* Blackstar ID:CORE V4: This amp has its own built-in preamp, power amp, and speakers designed to color the sound. Running a Valeton patch with its own Amp and Cab simulator turned ON into the front of the Blackstar creates a "double amp/double cab" effect. This results in an incredibly muddy, harsh, or boxy tone.
* PA System / Mixer: PA speakers are designed to project sound to a crowd over long distances. They push a lot of air and amplify harsh high frequencies (treble beam) and booming low ends, making headphone-tuned patches sound piercing or thin.

The Solution: Optimize the Valeton GP-200 for All Three
The Valeton GP-200 is highly capable of handling this exact workflow, but you need to configure your routing and global settings properly.

1. Tweaking for the Blackstar ID:CORE V4 (Home Practice)
Because the Blackstar is a guitar amp, you must avoid running the Valeton's cabinet models into it.
* The Connection: Plug the Valeton's Left/Mono Output into the Front Input of the Blackstar.
* Blackstar Settings: Set the Blackstar to the Clean Warm or Clean Bright voice. Turn the Gain down, and set the EQ (ISF) to a neutral position. Turn off all built-in Blackstar modulation, delay, and reverb effects.
* Valeton Setting: Turn OFF the CAB (Cabinet/IR) module on your patch. Let the Blackstar's physical speakers do the job. If you leave the Valeton Cab module on, it will sound deeply muffled.

2. Tweaking for the PA System / Mixer (Live Performance)
* The Connection: Use the XLR Outputs of the Valeton GP-200 to plug directly into the venue's mixer or stage snake.
* Valeton Setting: Turn ON the CAB/IR module. The PA requires the cabinet simulation to sound like a real guitar rig.

3. Pro-Tip: Use Global EQ and Separate Output Settings
Instead of creating different patches for home and live use, utilize the Valeton's hardware capabilities:
* Split the Outputs: In the Valeton's Global Settings, you can configure the 1/4" Jacks (to your Blackstar amp) to bypass the CAB module, while keeping the XLR Jacks (to the PA system) with the CAB module active.
* Use Global EQ at the Venue: Do not change your patch settings at the gig. If the PA sounds too bright or boomy, go to Global Settings > Global EQ and use the high/low filters to quickly cut out the muddy bass or harsh treble to match the venue's room acoustics.
